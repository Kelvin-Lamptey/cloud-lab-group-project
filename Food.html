Ramen

A Popular Japanese Noodle Dish

Ramen is a popular Japanese noodle dish known for its flavorful broth, noodles, and variety of toppings. It is enjoyed in Japan and many other countries around the world.

Origin

Ramen was introduced to Japan through Chinese noodle dishes in the late nineteenth century. Over time, it developed into a unique part of Japanese cuisine with many regional variations.

Today, different regions of Japan have developed their own ramen styles. Sapporo is known for miso ramen, Hakata for tonkotsu ramen, and Tokyo for shoyu ramen. These regional differences make ramen a diverse and interesting dish.

Anatomy of a Bowl

The main components of a traditional bowl of ramen are listed below.

  1. Noodles — Made from wheat flour, water, and kansui, which gives ramen noodles their firm texture and yellow color.
  2. Tare — A concentrated seasoning such as shoyu, shio, or miso that adds flavor to the broth.
  3. Broth — Usually prepared from pork bones, chicken, seafood, or vegetables and simmered for several hours.
  4. Ajitsuke Tamago — A soft-boiled egg marinated in soy sauce and other seasonings.
  5. Chashu — Thin slices of braised pork commonly served as a topping.
  6. Toppings — Ingredients such as nori, bamboo shoots, scallions, sesame seeds, and bean sprouts.

Regional Styles

Some of the most common regional ramen styles are shown below.

Style Region Broth Character
Tonkotsu Hakata, Fukuoka Pork bone, creamy & opaque Rich flavor with thin straight noodles.
Miso Sapporo, Hokkaido Miso-seasoned chicken or pork Hearty and warming, often served with corn and butter.
Shoyu Tokyo Clear soy-seasoned chicken broth Balanced flavor and one of the most common styles.
Shio Niigata & Hakodate Clear salt-seasoned seafood or chicken broth Light and delicate, highlighting the broth's natural taste.

How to Eat Ramen

  1. Serve the ramen while it is hot.
  2. Take a moment to enjoy the aroma of the broth.
  3. Taste the broth before adding any extra seasonings.
  4. Mix the ingredients gently.
  5. Eat the noodles while they are still hot.
  6. Use chopsticks and a spoon to enjoy the meal.
  7. Finish with the remaining broth if desired.
